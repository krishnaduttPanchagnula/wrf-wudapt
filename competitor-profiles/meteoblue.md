## Positioning & Messaging

**Primary value proposition (Observed)**: high-resolution visualisations of "temperature, wind, thermal comfort and pluvial flood risk" for city environments, delivered as maps and embeddable widgets.

**Positioning angle (Inferred, high confidence)**: visualization and communication layer for city climate — "prepare for heat, wind exposure and heavy rainfall impacts" — rather than an open counterfactual experiment tool.

**Key messaging themes (Observed)**:
- "Where small changes can make a big difference"; maps help "assess the impact of climate change mitigation measures, such as the targeted introduction of green spaces".
- Human-centred index: PET maps "provide a more human-centred view of urban heat stress than air temperature alone".
- Monitoring/observations: "For cities using local measurement networks, model data can be compared with ground observations to improve accuracy and monitor the effectiveness of adaptation measures over time."

### Core capabilities (Observed)
- Urban temperature maps, wind maps (2 m wind field animation), PET maps, pluvial flood information.
- 7-day forecast option and real-time precipitation radar overlay.

## Competitive Implications for wrf-wudapt

**Where they're strong vs. us**: map polish, breadth of indicators (PET, wind, pluvial flood), embeddability, city deployments.

**Where we're strong vs. them (Inferred, hypothesis)**: explicit counterfactual physics and a reproducible pipeline; we answer "what would happen if we changed the land use?" rather than only "where is it hot?".

**Opportunities**: adding PET/UTCI as a derived layer from our WRF output would close a visible communication gap; a small static comparison page would match the "close to you" map framing.

**Threats**: planners may expect ready-made maps rather than CSVs; our output interface is modest compared with these products.
